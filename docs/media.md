# Media — what there is, where it's used and what's missing

Client delivery of 2026-09-10, in `public/multimedia/`: **36 photos and 5
videos, 681 MB**.

That folder is the **input**, not what gets published. It's in `.gitignore` —same
as `public/fonts/_drop`— and what's versioned are the derivatives generated by
`npm run media`.

```
originals used      360.0 MB
derivatives           2.9 MB      −99.2 %
```

The reduction isn't just compression: the originals range from 7008×4672 to 4000×6000
and the derivatives are cropped to the size and aspect ratio at which they're displayed.

> ⚠️ **`next/image` isn't enough on its own.** It optimizes what it SERVES, but the
> source file in `public/` still ships with the deploy. With 681 MB of originals
> in there, the whole build carries that without anyone noticing.

| `Gaming Truck.mp4` | same, with the label "EVER GAME TRUCK" | ❌ it's a reel |

The three discarded ones are **social media pieces**. In a 130px card, muted and in a
6-second loop:

- the flyer can't be read, so nothing is left;
- the two reels are someone moving their mouth in silence, and that doesn't read as

**They're not deleted.** They stay in `public/multimedia/` and are useful for social media, or for an
events page with sound and playback controls. They're simply not
material for a decorative wall.

### What was done to the two that work

They were in **HEVC**, which only Safari plays — in Chrome and Firefox the
`<video>` stays black. They had to be re-encoded anyway.

```
9:16 → center crop to 3:4 → 480×640 → 6 s → H.264 → no audio → faststart
161 MB  →  440 KB        (live-music)
 98 MB  →  518 KB        (latin-night)
```

Each one also has a **poster** (~32 KB): it's the frame you see before the
video loads, and it's what's shown with `prefers-reduced-motion` instead
of the clip.

### 2 bis. The seven market photos, which are NOT from the delivery

⚠️ **The hero comes out at 2048 and not at the 2576 of the other six heroes.** The original
measures 2048×1365 —Wix doesn't serve anything larger, the untransformed URL is already the

### The hero photo

It's the largest on the site and the home page's LCP, so it gets its own section.

```
_RCZ5847.JPG   3465×5198  →  hero-truck-window.jpg  2576×1200  (506 KB)
focus 0.4586
```

**⚠️ `venue-aerial.jpg` used to be here, and it was a destination error.** That
derivative is generated at 1000×667 with quality 70 on purpose, because its place is the
background of the Events banner, which paints it with `blur-[22px]`. The hero showed it
sharp and full width: 1000px stretched to 2576 on a retina screen,
**2.6 times**, and no compression quality fixes an upscale. On top of that,
the aerial is of a yoga class and has JETSET's tents and banners,
meaning someone else's brand, which is what this same document lists as material
that doesn't work.

**Why 2576×1200 and not 2080 like the rest.** The frame measures 1288 CSS px wide
at most, so 2576 is exactly 2×: the first value that doesn't force
`next/image` to stretch on retina. With 2080 the ceiling is 1.61×. It's the only
photo that justifies the weight, because it's the only one at full width and with no blur to
forgive it. And the aspect ratio is the frame's to the pixel —2576/1200 = 1288/600—, so
the component's `object-cover` doesn't crop anything.

**Why that focus.** The photo is vertical and the band measures 1614 tall: there are
3584px left over, meaning the crop decides which photo it is. With the center at 0.4586 you get
the string lights, the lit window with the cook inside, the two people
ordering and the Birria Tacos sign. Higher up, the two people are cut at the
chest; lower down, an out-of-focus head appears at the bottom-left edge
that reads as a framing error.

**The other two finalists** were `_RCZ4830` (the backlit drinks) and
`_RCZ4336` (the yard at night). The drinks one fell out for two reasons: it's already
used in the `PhotoDivider`, and the orange of the "Together" plate washes out against
the amber of the drink. Over the blue of this one, the plate is the most saturated thing on the
first screen.

### Two notes on criteria

**Which photo you can put on an event.** The delivery doesn't include photos *of* these
events: it includes photos of the place. The rule applied is that the image shows
**the kind of night and the space** where the event happens, and that the `alt` describes
**what you see**, not what the title says. An `alt` that says "Bingo Night" over
a photo with no bingo in it is a lie to anyone using a screen reader.
The map lives in `IMAGES`, in `src/content/events.ts`.

**`truck-window-night.jpg` ended up unused.** It was generated for the gap in the
diagonal staircase of "Five businesses", and that section changed shape: now it's
three color squares with the greek-key pattern, no photos. The derivative is kept
because it's the best "order at whichever window you want" photo in the
delivery, and that section may want an image again.

⚠️ **But now it comes from the same original as the hero.** Before hanging it
anywhere you have to look at the whole home page: placed in a section, the same
scene would appear twice on the same page, which is exactly what was
avoided by choosing this photo for the hero and not the drinks one.

### Technical spec, for requesting them

- **Photos:** a 3000px long side is more than enough; the pipeline scales them down to
  what's displayed. JPEG. No filters or watermarks.
- **Videos:** vertical, 6–10 s, **no audio needed** (it's used muted),
  **no burned-in text**, **nobody talking to camera**. H.264 or HEVC —the
  pipeline re-encodes— and no size limit on the original.
- **What doesn't work:** Instagram screenshots, pieces with graphic labels, photos
  with another brand's logo on top.

## 4. The format: WebP, and the fifteen that aren't

Camila, 2026-09-22: "an optimization of all the images used in the
project, so they're in WebP format, without losing quality". The images
the site serves went from **17.95 MB to 15.24 MB (−15 %)** without any of them
dropping below the fidelity it already had. Fifteen stay in JPEG on purpose and the
reason is further below.

### ⚠️ This is NOT what makes the visitor receive WebP

That was already happening: `next.config.ts` declares `formats: ['image/avif', 'image/webp']`,
so everything that goes through `next/image` is served as AVIF or WebP converted on the
fly, whatever the file's format. What changes here is **the source
file**: the size of the repo and the deploy, the base from which `next/image`
converts, and —this one you do see— the files that are served raw, like the
bar sheets in the enlarged viewer of `MenuSheets`, which use an `<img>`.

### ⚠️ The measurement that decided everything, and the one that almost ruined it

The first comparison was the obvious one: convert the published JPEG to WebP and
compare. It said WebP needed quality 95 and **weighed twice as much**, and that
56 of 81 files got heavier. With that data the change would have been discarded entirely.

It was measured wrong. Comparing against the JPEG forces the WebP to reproduce the JPEG's
compression noise, which isn't the photo: it measures it against the rival's defects,
not against the original. Redone against the **uncompressed crop** —the only
honest reference— the answer flips: at equal fidelity measured by SSIM,
WebP saves up to 27 %, and 16 % across the pipeline as a whole.

That's why each file's quality is **measured one by one**, not picked by eye:
it's the minimum whose SSIM against the original matches or exceeds that of the JPEG that was
being published. It ranges from 60 to 92 and doesn't follow the JPEG's `q` — `join-stage-day-wide`
went down from 78 to 68 without losing anything and `contact-bar-day-wide` needed 86 to not
lose anything. The tables are in `build-media.mjs` and in `import-assets.mjs`.

### By group

| group | where it comes from | result |
|---|---|---|
| 52 from the pipeline | client's camera originals | all to WebP, **−16 %** |
| 25 from the old site | what Wix already recompressed | 17 to WebP **−12 %**, 6 stay |
| 4 menu sheets | downloaded from the old site | 3 to WebP, **−67 %** for Sea Me on Wheels |
| 4 video posters | first frame with ffmpeg | the 2 from the pipeline to WebP |

### ⚠️ The fifteen that stay in JPEG, and why it's not an oversight

They're the ones that **have no original**: the best version of them is already a JPEG that
Wix recompressed. When that JPEG came out nearly identical to its source (SSIM of 0.998
and up), matching it in WebP also requires reproducing the compression noise
Wix had already added, and there WebP weighs **more** than the JPEG. Converting them
would mean changing the format in exchange for size, a second lossy pass and
nothing in favor — that is, exactly the opposite of the request.

The original of each one was searched for among the 118 photos in `_interno/fotos/banco/` by
perceptual fingerprint. None has one: the closest candidates are
different photos. If the client delivers those originals, they get regenerated and go in.

### ⚠️ What was left out on purpose

- **`public/brand/greek-key.png`** (6 KB) is used as a `mask-image` in CSS. It saved
  1 KB and a mask is more delicate than a photo: it wasn't touched.
- **The ten loose originals in `public/images/venue/`** (40.5 MB in `DSC*`,
  `_RCZ*` and `WhatsApp*`) aren't served by any page: they only appear named in
  comments, as a record of which derivative came from which. Recompressing them
  would destroy the source from which the derivatives are regenerated. Their place is
  `public/multimedia/`, which is in `.gitignore`.

### How to verify nothing broke

All 17 routes of the site were walked, requesting every image they declare: 50
distinct files, none broken. And the three crops with the most aggressive quality
(`seafood-2` went down from 82 to 60) were compared side by side: indistinguishable, even
in the sea foam and in the plaid of a shirt, which is where a recompression
shows first.
## 5. The scripts

| command | what it does |
|---|---|
| `npm run media` | photos and videos: crops, re-encodes and generates posters |
| `npm run greek-key` | the two greek keys from the Figma → the pattern strip (148 KB → 6 KB) |
| `npm run horizon` | the transition element → the inline wave + the medallion |

All three read from `design-audit/tmp/` or from `public/multimedia/` and write to
`public/`. They're all idempotent: they can be run again with no side effects.

### Brand art brought in from the Figma on this branch

| piece | node | destination | size |
|---|---|---|---|
| greek key A and B | `2012:4489`, `2012:4488` | `public/brand/greek-key.png` | 148 KB → **6 KB** |
| horizon | `2012:4491` | `horizon-wave.ts` + `public/brand/sun-palm.svg` | 75 KB → 1.8 KB inline + 56 KB cached |

⚠️ Both scripts **rasterize or split** the SVGs instead of using them as
they come, and the reason is the same in both cases: they're hand-drawn paths with thousands
of micro-segments. The greek key is 8,601 and 10,375 numbers for a drawing that
repeats hundreds of times. The details are in the header of each script.
