# Measured contrast: `redesign/light-tropical`

Generated by `node scripts/contrast.mjs`. **The palette is read from
`src/app/globals.css`**, it isn't written in the script: this is the fix for the
problem the audit found, where `scripts/contrast.mjs` had the
palette copied by hand and it had gone stale, so every documented ratio
was calculated against colors the site didn't paint.

Method: WCAG 2.1 relative luminance. Minimums: **4.5:1** for body text,
**3:1** for large text (≥24px, or ≥19px bold) and for non-text user
interface components (WCAG 1.4.11). A decorative rule is not a user interface
component and has no minimum.

## The hard rules that come out of the table

1. **Orange and mid teal are not text colors on light.** They give
   2.54:1 and 2.50:1 on the base, and on the second surface
   even less (2.35:1). They don't even pass the large-text minimum, at
   any size. That's why the headline accent comes **in two colors depending on the
   surface**: `sunset-deep` on light, `sunset` on dark. Both
   are the SAME hue (28.3°) and the same saturation; only the
   luminosity changes between them, and that's why the accent reads orange on both surfaces.
2. **On an orange or teal fill, the text goes black.** White gives
   2.54:1 and 2.50:1; black gives 8.26:1 and 8.40:1.
3. **The headline accent is display-only, on both sides.** `sunset`
   on `deep` gives 4.26:1, and `sunset-deep` on `paper` gives
   3.54:1 (on `bone`, 3.28:1). Both pass the 3:1 for
   large text and neither passes the 4.5:1 for body: they work for one word in an
   88px headline, not for a subhead. The red `terra` (7.43:1) is the
   one that can go in body text, and it's the one used for form errors.
4. **The subhead has an opaque token, not an opacity.** `muted`
   `#487270` passes AA on all three light surfaces
   (5.36:1 · 4.96:1 · 4.67:1). With `text-body/75` the resulting
   color changed depending on whatever was behind it, so it couldn't be measured.
5. **The focus ring inverts on dark.** `ink` on `deep` would be
   1.58:1: invisible. The `.on-dark` rule in globals.css switches it to
   `cream`, which gives 9.44:1.

## Composite colors: what this table CANNOT predict

A translucent color over another translucent color over a photo can't be resolved
here: it only shows up by measuring the rendered pixel. The known case is the `video`
label on the hero wall, which is translucent `paper` over a translucent
fill. **It's verified with `npm run a11y` on the served page**, not in
this table.

With the light direction that label becomes opaque `muted` on opaque
`paper`, that is, 5.36:1, measurable here, which is one of the reasons why
it's better for the wall to stop relying on opacities.
